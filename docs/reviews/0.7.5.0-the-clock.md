## F60, closed: what was wrong, established before the fix

0.7.0.0 decided that expiry moves to the wall clock and named the cost (F47).
`grant.py`'s header and the manual said so. Every wiring site - `cli.py`
twice, `mcp/pump.py` once - built its issuer with `time.monotonic`, from
0.7.0.0 through 0.7.4.0. The finding as filed at 0.7.3.0 was about contract:
CPython leaves the monotonic reference point undefined outside one process,
and the comparison held on Linux and macOS by implementation detail. That
was the smallest of three problems.

- **Reboot** [measured, against `201608e` with the injected clock]. A grant
  issued with the clock at 1 000 000 and spent with it at 50 was accepted:
  a deadline written in one boot epoch looks far in the future in the next,
  so an unspent grant file outlived its TTL by up to the previous uptime.
  The production wiring wrote `issued_at` 330.3 against `time.time()`
  1 790 282 440.8 in the review container, which is uptime-scale by any
  reading.
- **Sleep** [documented, not measured on a host]. CPython 3.14 implements
  `time.monotonic` with `mach_absolute_time()` on macOS (read in
  `Python/pytime.c` on the 3.14 branch), which Apple documents as not
  advancing while the system sleeps; on Linux, `CLOCK_MONOTONIC` does not
  count time the system is suspended (man-pages, `clock_getres.2`). A grant
  approved before a lid closes could be spent after it opens as though no
  time had passed. This path was not in F60 as filed, and it is the one an
  operator on a laptop actually meets.
- **Contract** [documented]. As filed.

## What changed

- **Deadlines on the wall clock, chosen once.** `GrantIssuer.on_wall_clock`
  is the only production constructor, and all three wiring sites use it. The
  `clock` parameter survives as the test seam. A19 still holds - the wiring
  site builds the issuer and picks its key and store - but the clock is no
  longer a choice it has (A22).
- **A spend clock before issuance is refused** as
  `refusal.grant_not_yet_valid`, after the target check and before both the
  expiry check and the burn. Burning first would turn the refusal into a lost
  grant; mutant M7 below is that ordering, and sixteen tests kill it.
  `issued_at` is inside the MAC scope, so the check cannot be dodged by
  editing the file.
- **Durations stay monotonic.** The attention interval and the proposal age
  are differences inside one process, and a wall-clock step must not be able
  to shorten a reading floor. Nothing in `approve.py` or `gate_approve.py`
  changed.
- **Upgrade behaviour, measured.** A grant written by 0.7.4.0 carries
  uptime-scale instants under a MAC that still verifies. The wall-clock
  issuer refuses it as expired with an `overdue_seconds` above a billion,
  pinned by `test_a_grant_from_the_monotonic_era_is_refused_as_expired` and
  described in MANUAL section 16.

## PT21, and the acceptance that does not hold

One backward step is either larger than the time the grant has lived, and
refused, or no larger, and the grant lives longer by that step: under one
extra TTL. Repeated small steps are not bounded, and PT21 accepts them with
a trigger.

The tempting argument for that acceptance - whoever can set the clock can
read the key file, and the key mints any grant outright - is true for root
and false for the time daemon, which holds the privilege to set the clock
without the privilege to read the operator's home, and for anyone who feeds
it a false time over unauthenticated NTP. PT21 therefore rests on what a
step cannot change: it keeps an approved action spendable longer, and it
cannot alter the bytes, the target, or single use. Recorded here so the
shorter argument is not re-imported from a first draft of the reasoning.

## Deliberately not done

- **A second clock in the issuer** (wall clock authoritative, monotonic as a
  same-process cross-check): rejected. It adds a second test seam for a
  check that would fire only inside `pirx run`, where issue and spend are
  seconds apart, and the not-before check already bounds a backward step
  without it.
- **A tolerance on the not-before check**: none. A spend refused by a small
  time-sync step costs one re-approval; a tolerance widens PT21's residual
  to buy convenience nobody has measured a need for.
- **A control for PT21**: owned by its trigger - issuer and spender on
  different clocks, or the first action whose effect depends on when it
  runs.
